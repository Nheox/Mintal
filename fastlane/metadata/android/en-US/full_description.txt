Stay focused, stay mintalized.

Mintal is a focus timer built around real plants. Choose how long you want to
concentrate, start the timer, and leave your phone alone. Finish the block and the
session is planted in your Grove — a bed of mints that grows one node at a time and
becomes the record of your attention.

Every plant is a real species of Mentha, drawn from its actual field marks: spearmint's
pointed serrated leaf, apple mint's white-woolly pucker, Corsican mint's creeping mat
of tiny round leaves. Twelve of them unlock as your focused hours accumulate, and each
milestone lets you choose which one you want next.

FEATURES

* Countdown sessions from 5 minutes to 3 hours, or count up like a stopwatch
* The Grove: one session is one node, twelve nodes is a mature plant, and the next
  session starts another beside it
* Twelve Mentha species, each with a botanical note, unlocked by cumulative focus time
* Leave the app and a visible fifteen-second warning gives you time to come back
* An allowlist, so checking a dictionary does not end a session
* Tags with colours, an optional note, and a 1-5 rating for every session
* Statistics: totals, best focus day, best focus hour, time per tag, rating over time
* Export everything as CSV, or back up and restore the whole database as JSON
* Light and dark themes

PRIVACY, PLAINLY

Mintal declares no INTERNET permission at all. Not "we promise not to look" — the app
cannot open a network connection, and you can verify that in the manifest. There is no
account, no sign-in, no sync, no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, and no
Google Play Services. Everything lives in a database on your device, and you can export
or delete all of it whenever you like.

Nothing is for sale. There is no premium tier, no cosmetic purchase, and no species
locked behind a price. Every feature is available to everyone.

Mintal asks for four permissions, all of them implied by "a timer that keeps running":
foreground service, notifications, wake lock and vibrate. Two further permissions are
optional, explained in Mintal's own words before Android asks, and the app works fully
if you refuse them.
